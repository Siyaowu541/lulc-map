How are percentages calculated?

Every paper counts once within the selected study year, or once in All years. A paper covering multiple countries is divided equally between them; its share is then divided equally between the intersecting provinces/states and local study regions. The denominators are shown as paper-equivalents, so percentages sum to 100% without counting one paper several times. Global studies and unlocated/distributed datasets are separate in the world chart. Continental and country-wide studies stay in their own categories at finer levels. These are literature shares, not land area, land-cover composition, sample density or data availability. Chart shares use the complete year-filtered collection, not only the visible viewport. Groups below 3% may be combined as Other; expand Other to see them. Zoom thresholds: below 3 = continents; 3–5 = countries; 5–8 = provinces/states; 8 and above = local study regions.

How coverage is interpreted

The selected year is the year represented by a paper’s land-cover results or reference imagery, not its publication year. For example, a paper published in 2025 with annual maps for 2000–2020 appears when 2002 is selected. Separate observations in 2004, 2014 and 2024 appear only in those three years; gaps are not filled. Later publications remain eligible when they describe the selected year. Unreported years and synthetic scenes appear only under All years. Mixed-year composites are excluded from a single-year view unless you enable them; they are not annual maps.

Boundaries remain research outlines as you zoom in; they do not turn into pixel-level classified data. Areas represent the geographic scope of the reviewed papers, not data-download availability or a count of publications. Solid green shapes use image bounds, published tile outlines or a named regional boundary. Orange dashed shapes use administrative context, an alternative regional definition, or an approximate outline digitised from a paper figure. A city outline can be larger than the imagery actually analysed.
